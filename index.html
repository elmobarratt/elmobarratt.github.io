About Me

I am a first-year PhD student in Computer Science at UCL and a Martingale Foundation Scholar, supervised within Prof Andrew Saxe's Theory of Learning Lab at the Gatsby Computational Neuroscience Unit. My research sits at the intersection of computational neuroscience and theoretical machine learning, with a particular focus on continual learning, representational geometry, and how the structure of sensory representations shapes learning, transfer and interference in biological and artificial neural systems.

Before beginning my PhD, I joined the Gatsby Unit as a predoctoral research intern, where I developed theoretical and empirical approaches to studying continual learning across neural networks and animal behaviour. My background is in mathematics, and I am especially interested in using dynamical systems, statistical learning theory and representation-based approaches to understand how learning unfolds over time. Before returning to research, I spent two years as a Technical Solutions Engineer at Epic Systems working on healthcare software and clinical decision-support systems.

Projects

Continual Learning, Sensory Geometry and Animal Behaviour - Gatsby Unit

This project asks how the geometry of sensory representations determines what is learned, what generalises and what is forgotten when tasks change. In a nonlinear shallow neural network trained by online stochastic gradient descent on classification tasks defined over a circular stimulus manifold, we derive large-input-dimension learning dynamics in which prediction errors propagate across stimuli according to their representational similarity kernel.


We apply the same framework to an auditory categorisation dataset in which rats learn changing binary decision boundaries over snippets from a repeating song. Pairwise acoustic similarity provides an empirical, non-stationary stimulus kernel, allowing us to ask whether the same geometric quantities that govern learning in the artificial network can explain structured transfer and forgetting in animal behaviour. A central goal is to develop task-similarity measures that respect the underlying sensory representation rather than depending only on task labels or boundary separation.

Masters Thesis - An ACO Algorithm and its Application to the Travelling Salesman Problem

Paper and GitHub Repo.


I independently investigated an Ant Colony Optimisation algorithm for approximate solutions to the shortest Hamiltonian cycle on complete random Euclidean graphs. Research Interests

Continual Learning & Transfer: understanding how task similarity, interference and representational structure determine transfer, forgetting and adaptation across sequential learning.

Representation Learning & Geometry: studying how learned and sensory representations shape generalisation, with particular interest in similarity kernels, task geometry and structured stimulus manifolds.

Theoretical Learning Dynamics: mathematical descriptions of neural-network learning using dynamical systems, online learning theory, kernel methods and statistical mechanics-inspired approaches.

Computational Neuroscience: connecting theories of learning in artificial networks to neural and behavioural data, particularly where biological learning provides constraints or phenomena that current machine-learning theory does not naturally explain.
