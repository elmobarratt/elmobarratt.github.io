About Me

I am an MSci Mathematics graduate from the University of Bristol, driven by a fascination with how complex emergent structure arises from simple rules, whether in agentic systems, fractal geometry or neural representations. My interest in machine learning began at 16 when I first encountered images from Gatys et al.’s neural style transfer, which revealed to me how profoundly computation could become creativity. After my degree and two years as a Technical Solutions Engineer at Epic, where I saw firsthand the importance of interpretable and uncertainty-aware AI in clinical decision support, I took a year to travel solo across Asia. The freedom from responsibility and immersion in other people’s ways of living taught me a lot about independence, my tendencies toward mischievous curiosity and comfort with uncertainty. But after a long year of not wearing shoes, my hunger to pursue my own intellectual purpose has been growing strong.

I now aim to become a researcher in theoretical and biologically inspired machine learning, with particular interest in how neural networks learn structure through local signals, how probabilistic principles shape intelligence and how biophysical data such as EEG or fMRI can be decoded through multimodal embedding spaces. I hope to join a collaborative, mathematically rigorous PhD environment surrounded by supportive peers, postdocs, and mentors and while I’m open to industry research internships, my long-term trajectory lies in academia.

Masters Thesis – An ACO Algorithm and its Application to The Travelling Salesman Problem

Paper and Github Repo.

In this project I independently investigated a meta-heuristic Ant Colony Optimisation (ACO) algorithm for finding approximate solutions to the shortest Hamiltonian cycle on a complete random Euclidean graph. LIF Spiking Neural Network to Simulate EEG-like Extracellular Fields

Github Repo


I built a leaky integrate-and-fire (LIF) spiking neural network on a random Euclidean graph to simulate stable, EEG-like extracellular fields. Using the 3D spatiotemporal activity tensor (2D space × time), I trained convolutional neural networks in tensorflow.keras to infer structural properties of the underlying graph. The model successfully recovered the spatial connectivity constant with high confidence, demonstrating that CNNs can extract latent connectivity features directly from simulated extracellular field dynamics. Future work on this would include synaptic plasticity to dynamically change the weights connecting neurones as the simulation evolves.

Local Learning Rules for Kolmogorov–Arnold Networks (KANs)
Github Repo

Developing biologically inspired, synaptically local training algorithms for spline-based neural architectures.

I am investigating how to train Kolmogorov–Arnold Networks (KANs) using local learning rules rather than global backpropagation. KANs represent each synapse as a learnable univariate spline, giving them strong interpretability and naturally local parameterisation, yet their training remains fully dependent on global gradients.


The project explores three complementary directions:

  • Prospective Configuration-style Hebbian learning for KANs: I derive energy-based inference dynamics for KAN layers and develop Hebbian updates that adjust spline coefficients using only locally available prediction errors and pre/post-synaptic activity. This enables stable, fully local learning without backprop.
  • Temporal and causal structure via spiking mechanisms: I incorporate ideas from STDP and spiking neural networks to give KANs a temporal learning rule. By treating spline activations as continuous dendritic responses modulated by spike timing, the model can learn unsupervised structure in temporal or event-based data.
  • Local Bayesian updates for uncertainty-aware KANs: I design local approximate Bayesian updates where each spline coefficient maintains a simple prior and performs inner-loop local inference, providing uncertainty estimates.
